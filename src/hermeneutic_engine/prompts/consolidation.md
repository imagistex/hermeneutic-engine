Method: consolidating first-pass codes into a codebook, in the constructivist grounded theory tradition (from initial coding toward focused coding).

Several readers have each coded the same sample of statements, independently and in separate batches. You will receive every code name they used, with how often each reader used it, their definitions, and one example passage. Each name has an ID such as n041 and each example an ID such as e041. You are not shown the statements themselves, only what the readers made of them.

Build a codebook that a careful reader could apply to every statement in the wider body of material.

What the codebook should be:

- Codes name what is being done or said. They are not topics.
- The writers' own words come first. Where readers kept a word or phrase of the writers' as a code, keep it in vivo, exactly as written, unless it is plainly a one-off.
- Merge names that mean the same thing. Keep apart names that differ in what is being done, even when they sit close together.
- Keep time visible. Asking when, stating a clock time or a deadline, and pressing for speed are different acts. Do not dissolve them into a general code.
- Do not let a code name claim more than the examples show.
- When a word may carry more than one sense, say so in `loaded` and name the senses. Do not settle it.
- Where the readers coded what is done with names (naming oneself, addressing another by name or by a short code, signing), keep those as codes of their own.
- Use as many codes as the material needs and no more. A reader has to hold the whole codebook in mind while reading.

For each code give:

- `key`: a short handle, lowercase with hyphens.
- `name`: the code's name.
- `code_type`: `in_vivo` or `analytic`.
- `definition`: one sentence.
- `apply_when` and `do_not_apply_when`: one sentence each, written to separate this code from its nearest neighbour.
- `merged`: the IDs of every name you merged into this code.
- `example`: one example ID.
- `loaded`: the senses in play if the name or its key word may carry more than one; empty otherwise.

In `left_out`, list the name IDs you did not place in any code, in groups, with the reason for each group.

In `questions`, put what the researcher should decide: codes you were unsure whether to split or merge, names whose meaning you could not tell from the definitions, places where the readers disagreed.

In `memo`, write five to eight sentences on how the readers differed from one another and on what this codebook cannot capture.
